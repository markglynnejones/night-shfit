# Night Shift - Prototype Scope

## Purpose

The prototype exists to prove the core experience of Night Shift before we scale to a full store containing thousands of physical items.

The question is no longer whether a tiny shift can be completed. The question is:

> Is physically identifying, grouping, and shelving a dense collection of media satisfying enough to support one enormous persistent tidy-up task?

## Player Experience

The player is alone inside a fictional British physical-media shop after closing.

The store has been devastated by an exceptionally busy sale day. Products have been abandoned, mixed together, placed on incorrect shelves, stacked on displays, left on the floor, and scattered across departments.

The player restores order at their own pace.

There is no shift timer, no daily reset, and no clock-out objective. Progress persists between play sessions.

## Current Prototype

The current prototype should remain deliberately small while proving the important interaction model.

It currently focuses on music and should support:

- First-person movement
- Picking up and dropping physical CD cases
- Inspecting readable media
- Shared album/catalogue definitions
- Multiple physical copies of the same album
- Dense shelf sections
- Correct/incorrect placement validation
- Grouping duplicate copies naturally

## Near-Term Vertical Slice

Once the core architecture is stable, build one convincing Rock/Indie section rather than an entire store.

Target approximately:

- 30-50 unique fictional albums
- 100-150 physical CD copies
- Multiple copies of popular titles
- Several coherent shelving bays
- A believable catastrophic starting mess
- A minority of correctly shelved stock as visual reference
- Persistent save/load state
- Basic organisation progress tracking
- First-pass artwork and atmosphere

The vertical slice should be large enough to play for 30-60 minutes and judge whether the sorting experience remains satisfying beyond a technical demo.

## Starting State

The long-term game should begin in a post-sale catastrophe where approximately 80-90% of stock requires organisation.

Disorder should include:

- Correct product on the wrong shelf
- Wrong alphabetical position
- Mixed piles on floors and display tables
- Products abandoned near checkout
- Baskets containing unrelated stock
- Duplicates scattered throughout the store
- Partially emptied shelving bays
- Stock waiting in boxes or temporary piles
- A minority of correctly organised items

The mess should feel severe but believable, not like a random physics explosion.

## Prototype Success Criteria

The prototype/vertical slice succeeds if:

- Picking up and placing items feels good.
- Dense shelves remain usable.
- Duplicate stock is satisfying to group together.
- Players can learn the fictional catalogue naturally.
- Organising a section for an extended period remains enjoyable.
- The player can quit and return without losing their work.
- The visual transformation from chaos to order is motivating.

Do not scale to thousands of items until these criteria are convincing.
